Understanding My "I'm Not Good Enough" Pattern

Sometimes our mind develops a core belief about ourselves early in life. It then creates 'rules for living' that promise to protect us from feeling that belief.  
  
Core Belief:  
“I’m not good enough.”  
  
This worksheet is about noticing how this belief influences your daily choices—not judging yourself for it.

Part 3: What Was I Afraid Would Happen?

Complete:  
If I didn't follow this rule...  
I’ll perceive myself as someone who was unable to make it. The difficulty made me feel like some things were outside my control but since I was trying I was good enough.  
  
And if that happened...  
If I failed it would mean that I didn’t deserve all these things that I got.  
  
And that would mean...  
I’m lying to myself about my potential and the gap between my perception of myself and the reality are radically different  
  
What does it ultimately say about me?  
I care more about my perception of my life than my actual life.

Part 4: The Cost of Following This Rule

Short-term benefits:  
Ability to take risks other people would be too anxious to attempt.  
  
Long-term costs  
Emotionally:  
Constant dialogue between real and perceived self  
  
Physically:  
Stress and extra effort for emergencies  
  
Relationships:  
Not able to fully open up about my problems because I feel they won’t understand me.  
  
Career/Creativity:  
Long gaps in career, where I can only build breadth but not depth.

Part 6: Whose Voice Is This?

Who does this rule sound like?  
Someone who intellectualises their suffering

When I was having trouble fitting in and was influenced by nihilist media  
  
What experiences reinforced it?  
The times when I was able to get to something nice by struggling for it. Especially in 2nd year of undergrad.

  

Part 7: If My Worth Was Never in Question...

Imagine you already believed: 'I am enough even when I'm imperfect.'  
  
At work:  
I’ll have less executive dysfunction and generally be able to spend more time on creating things than trying to sound smart.  
  
With rest:  
I’ll be able to be more present in situations where I’m supposed to relax.

  
With relationships:  
Be able to love people for who they are and not what I project on them.

  
With yourself:  
Live a life aligned with my values where I feel fulfilled.

  

Part 8: A Compassionate Reframe

I need to do certain things to feel valued and worthy.

I am enough as I am.
